## Observable production grammar

Frame sampling of the public video shows:

- a high-key white canvas with soft blue/cyan translucent ribbons;
- a quiet "Introducing" opening and restrained brand reveal;
- a centered natural-language prompt box with typed input and suggestion chips;
- progress/checklist states that make the system's work visible;
- product UI shown first as a full window, then through controlled zooms;
- animated cursor travel and click-led data reveals;
- alternating sparse claim frames and detailed product frames;
- a large isolated database-size proof point;
- workflow/integration moments with recognizable app tiles;
- a Claude prompt/result moment that connects product use to agentic work;
- a minimal product end card.

The design avoids a montage feel. It behaves like one continuous camera moving
between a spacious brand world and sharp UI evidence.

These are surface observations from one finished artifact. They do not recover:

- the original prompt or iteration history;
- shot-by-shot timing, easing, camera coordinates, or transition geometry;
- typography tokens, source assets, component architecture, or brand system;
- sound design decisions or the relationship between audio accents and edits;
- rejected directions or the criteria used to choose the final cut.

Matching ribbons, prompt boxes, cursors, rounded windows, or a blue-white palette
therefore does not establish meaningful similarity.

## Classification

### Transferable production practices

- use deterministic frame-driven animation;
- make system work visible when that is part of the product story;
- alternate context and detail to preserve comprehension;
- use cursor or focus motion only to explain a real action;
- hold evidence long enough to read;
- maintain continuity across one timeline.

These practices are useful only when they fit the selected video archetype and
the demonstrated product behavior.

### Optional treatment attributes

- high-key white field;
- blue/cyan translucent ribbons;
- quiet introducing beat;
- centered prompt entry;
- rounded product windows;
- cursor-led close-ups;
- isolated proof metric;
- minimal branded end card.

Apply these only after the story and product-task contracts are locked.

### Unknown and unrecoverable decisions

Do not infer exact pacing, animation curves, component code, prompt wording,
sound rhythm, asset generation process, or rationale from the published video.
When similarity matters, create an explicit attribute map for the new brief and
evaluate against that map rather than claiming to reproduce hidden decisions.
Use eight dimensions: shot scale, edit pacing, camera continuity, composition,
typography, source-asset fidelity, transitions, and audio rhythm. Mark a
dimension `unknown` when this study does not support it. Do not fill evidence
gaps with a motif or generic adjective.

## Safe reference-treatment principle

When a user asks for "the Tim Kochjar prompt" or "that style":

1. disclose that the exact prompt is not public;
2. lock the user's story and product behavior before choosing any treatment;
3. translate observable qualities into a treatment-specific attribute map;
4. use the user's own brand, product, claims, assets, and CTA;
5. create original scene choreography instead of tracing the video shot for
   shot;
6. cite this study when provenance matters;
7. say `reference-derived treatment`, not `Tim Kochjar-style`, when reporting the
   result.
